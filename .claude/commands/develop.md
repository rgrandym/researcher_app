Develop this draft: $ARGUMENTS

Read `.claude/skills/compose/SKILL.md` and follow it, with `kind = piece`, and read the `piece` entry in
its `references/kinds.md`.

The author's draft is the starting text. Their concepts, their order and their
emphasis all survive into the result; what you add is the prose that makes the
argument land. A developed draft that reads better and no longer says what they
said has failed the task.

Before writing, say back in two lines what you take their argument to be and
who you take the reader to be. A wrong reading costs a whole round.

Where the draft asserts something about the world that no source backs, flag it
in chat and leave it standing. Where the author asks you to check it, §7 of the
`compose` skill applies: scout the claim, record the evidence, report in chat,
and keep the marker out of the text.
