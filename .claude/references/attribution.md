# Single sources and attribution

Shared doctrine. `report`, `verify`, `repair`, `critique`, `research` and the
scout/verifier agents all point here.

**A claim resting on one source is not a defective claim.** Most real findings
rest on one study, one filing, one dataset — that is the normal state of
evidence, not a failure of research. The defect is never the count. The defect is
**presenting one source in the narrator's voice as though it were settled**.

The fix is therefore attribution, not deletion, not hedging, and not a downgrade
into vagueness.

## Four situations, only two of which are problems

| Situation | Defect? | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| One source, sound grade, honestly the only one located | **No** | Attribute in text: *"one study reports…"*, *"in the only located survey, the authors report…"* |
| One source, low grade (vendor, blog, opinion) | Partly | Attribute **and** name the class: *"the vendor states…"*, *"one consultancy reports…"* |
| Several citations that collapse to one origin | **Yes** | `CIRCULAR`. Collapse to the origin and attribute once. The defect is manufactured corroboration |
| One source written as consensus — *"the market saw…"*, *"studies show…"* | **Yes** | The voice is the defect, not the sourcing. Restore the attribution |

## The standard forms

Scientific register, in order of increasing distance from the narrator:

- *"Chen et al. report X [E7]."* — one study, sound, uncontested.
- *"In the only located head-to-head comparison, X differed 3.6-fold [E724][E725]."* — signals the evidence base is a single study without disparaging it.
- *"One industry survey puts the figure at 7% [E1060]."* — one source, secondary class.
- *"The manufacturer states X [E406]."* — interested party, never laundered.
- *"A single consultancy source gives 30–50%; the primary chapter it cites could not be accessed [E802]."* — one source, low grade, and the trail is broken.

Consultancy register uses the same forms; it is not more permissive about voice.

**The narrator's voice — a bare declarative with no attribution — is reserved for
what multiple independent sources agree on.** That is the only thing the plain
form means, and spending it on a single source is what makes a report
untrustworthy in ways a reader cannot see.

## What this means for each stage

- **`research`** — `THIN` records that a claim has one source. It is a prompt to
  ask *is one enough here?*, not an automatic re-scout. Re-scout when the claim is
  load-bearing and a second independent source plausibly exists; otherwise record
  it and let the report attribute it.
- **`report`** — attribute in the sentence. The `[E#]` tag alone does not tell a
  reader how many sources stand behind a claim, and they will not go and count.
- **`verify`** — `WEAK` means *low-grade or unreliable*, not *singular*. A single
  A1 primary that plainly states the claim is `SUPPORTED`. Where a verdict turns
  on the evidence being singular, say so in the Fix line as an attribution
  instruction, not as a downgrade.
- **`repair`** — the repair for single-sourcing is to add the attribution, not to
  soften the claim. Turning a sourced number into "significant" destroys
  information and fixes nothing.
- **`critique`** — a single-sourced claim is a finding **only** when it is
  unattributed, load-bearing and presented as settled, or when the source is
  interested. "Only one source" alone is not a finding.

## Where the count does have to be visible

Attribution handles the sentence. Two places still carry the count explicitly:

- **Limitations** — the weakest load-bearing source, named, with its grade.
- **The confidence statement** — never High on single-origin evidence, however
  many outlets repeated it.
